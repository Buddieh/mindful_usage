Many walls built after the Second World War have a cavity between two layers of brick. Insulation is injected into it from outside, without changing the facade. A test drill in a joint tells you a lot: if the drill breaks through after about 10 cm, there is a cavity. Such walls are usually at least 24 cm thick.

An installer then checks the cavity with a camera. It must be at least 5 cm wide and not full of mortar or brick debris. Insulating is not advisable with frost damage, cracks, an outer layer that blocks vapour (such as glazed brick or some paints), or a facade exposed to driving rain.

You can choose any contractor, who stays liable for the work for 10 years. Premiums require a contractor on the official list of certified installers, so check the current conditions first.
