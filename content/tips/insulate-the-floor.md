How you insulate a floor depends on what is underneath. Above a cellar or crawl space, boards are fixed to the underside of the floor and you don't need to break anything open. A floor laid directly on the ground is harder: the covering, screed and subfloor have to come out first, so it is best done when the floor is being replaced anyway.

Insulating on top of an existing floor is possible but works less well. It needs pressure-resistant boards, raises the floor level, and doors or windows may need adjusting.

Solve any damp problem before you start. As a guide, the Flemish government considers about 5 cm of sprayed PUR/PIR or 7 cm of mineral wool good practice today; to meet the 2050 target above a cellar, aim for about 7 cm of PUR/PIR or 10 cm of mineral wool.
