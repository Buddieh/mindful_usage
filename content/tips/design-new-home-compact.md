Some energy choices for a new home are made on the drawing board. A Flemish building guide for near-zero-energy homes gives three pointers.

Keep the shape compact. Compactness compares the heated volume with the area of the shell that loses heat. The more compact the home, the easier it is to insulate and the less heat it loses.

Put the main windows to the east, south or west. They let in free heat from the winter sun, but they also warm the rooms quickly in summer, so plan sun shading on those three sides. It doesn't have to be movable or automatic.

Choose ventilation with demand control or heat recovery. Ventilation system C lets fresh air in naturally and extracts it mechanically, while system D both supplies and extracts air mechanically. In the EPB calculation, systems that avoid ventilating more than needed or that recover heat score better on energy.
