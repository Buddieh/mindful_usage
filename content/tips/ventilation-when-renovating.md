Older homes are often ventilated by accident, through gaps and leaks. Insulating and sealing closes those gaps, so moisture, smells and stale air stay inside unless you ventilate in a controlled way. A household produces about 10 to 20 litres of moisture a day.

The principle is simple: fresh air comes into the dry rooms (living room, bedrooms), flows under the doors, and is extracted from the wet rooms (kitchen, bathroom, toilet). It should run day and night; a cooker hood alone is not enough.

System C brings fresh air in naturally through grilles and extracts it with fans. System D supplies and extracts air with fans and can recover heat from the outgoing air. Demand control, which ventilates more when rooms are in use, also limits the heat lost.
