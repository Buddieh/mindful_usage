Breathing wood smoke causes coughing, headaches and stinging eyes, and it can also lead to more serious harm such as heart and vascular disease. An open fireplace, or a stove without a self-closing door, also has a very low efficiency and is open to the air you breathe indoors. The VMM advises taking it out of use.

If you do burn wood, let it dry for at least two years in a dry place, and light the fire from the top. Never close the air supply of the stove, which also creates a carbon monoxide risk, and never burn waste, plastic or treated wood. An automatically controlled pellet stove is cleaner than a stove for logs.

Don't light a fire on foggy or windless days, or when a burning advisory (stookadvies) has been issued.
