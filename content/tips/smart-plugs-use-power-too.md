A smart plug stays connected to your Wi-Fi so you can reach it from your phone, and that costs power. In a 2016 measurement study, Wi-Fi smart plugs drew between 2.6 and 4.1 W around the clock. Newer models may use less, but it adds up for a plug that is always on. A plain energy-saving plug draws about 0.1 to 0.3 W, and a power strip with a switch draws nothing when it is off.

Smart plugs are useful when you want to shift or schedule something, for example starting an appliance remotely or at a set time, which can help keep your capacity peak low. A simple mechanical timer does the same for appliances that only need fixed hours. For fire safety, let appliances run only when someone is at home.

To cut standby, a switched power strip is usually the cheapest and most reliable choice.
