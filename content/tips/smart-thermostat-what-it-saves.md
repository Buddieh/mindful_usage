Manufacturers often promise savings of 25 to 40%, but those figures rest on unrealistic assumptions. A 2018 review of the research found moderate-quality evidence that smart thermostats do not save energy compared with standard thermostats, and a UK government review found little or no saving from programmable thermostats in practice, partly because most people rarely set a schedule. If you already turn the heating down at night and when you're out, a smart thermostat will add little, and comfort features can even raise your use.

What does save energy is how the heating is set. The Flemish government advises a digital thermostat with at least two programs: turn it down to 14 to 16 °C an hour before bed and when the house is empty during the day. Thermostatic radiator valves let you control each room separately and pay for themselves in under two years. Zone control per room can save energy when it is set up properly.

A smart thermostat can still be worth it if it gets you to use a schedule you otherwise wouldn't, or if you want remote control. In a poorly insulated home, insulation will save far more.
