An energy management system (EMS) is a small device or app that connects to the user port of your digital meter. It decides when appliances run and when energy is stored, for example in a heat-pump boiler, an electric car or a home battery, so that more of your solar power is used at home.

That helps in two ways. You buy less power from the grid, and you avoid peaks, which matters because part of your network costs depends on your highest quarter-hour peak each month. With a dynamic electricity contract, an EMS can also shift use to cheaper hours.

You don't always need a separate system. Many heat pumps and electric boilers have smart control or a "smart-grid ready" function that your installer can switch on; a timer that heats the boiler water at midday also helps when you have solar panels. Watch out for a boiler's backup heating element, which can push your peak up sharply. No official source gives typical savings, so compare the cost of a system with what you could realistically shift.
